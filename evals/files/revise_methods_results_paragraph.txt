It should be noted that in order to examine the association between traffic density and emergency department visits for asthma, a spatial lag model was utilized, which was estimated by means of maximum likelihood, and in which a number of covariates were included for the purpose of controlling for the fact that counties differ with respect to socioeconomic composition, and it was found that the coefficient on traffic density was positive and significant. The implementation of the spatial weights matrix was based on queen contiguity — first-order neighbors only — and row standardization was performed prior to the estimation of the model. The data was obtained from the Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project (HCUP) for the years 2015 to 2019. Model comparison was conducted using the Akaike information criterion, and it was determined that the spatial lag specification was superior in comparison to the ordinary least squares specification (AIC of 1,482 and 1,536, respectively). This indicates that there may potentially be a spillover effect that could possibly suggest that traffic exposure in neighboring counties is also of relevance. The magnitude of the direct effect was 0.14 (95% CI 0.06–0.22) and the magnitude of the indirect effect was 0.09 (95% CI 0.01–0.17), which is a very interesting finding that is highly significant in terms of its policy implications.
